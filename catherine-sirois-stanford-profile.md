## Research

Her research examines how the state governs marginalized groups — particularly low-income Black and Brown families — across institutional interactions and population-level policy. Core areas include:

- How the state manages children at the junction of child welfare and juvenile justice institutions ("crossover youth")
- How the reentry process after incarceration shapes social and economic well-being
- Effects of state policy (mass incarceration, restrictive immigration law) on marginalized families

Methods span ethnographic observation, in-depth interviewing, and quantitative/causal-inference analysis of survey and administrative data. Her work touches poverty and inequality, punishment and society, race/class/gender, health disparities, and methods for studying hard-to-reach populations.

Published in: *American Sociological Review*, *American Journal of Sociology*, *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, *Social Forces*, *American Journal of Epidemiology*, *Sociological Forum*, *Social Science & Medicine*.

**Current book project:** *Interlocking Institutions: Welfare, Punishment, and the Governance of Crossover Youth* — based on 300+ hours of ethnographic observation in a California juvenile court and 40 interviews with court actors. Supported by NSF, the Josephine de Karman Foundation, and Stanford funding sources. A related article in *American Sociological Review* received the **Devah Pager Outstanding Article Award** (ASA Inequality, Poverty, and Mobility Section).

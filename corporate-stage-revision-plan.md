## 1. Clarify and justify the Goffman/Foucault/Zuboff synthesis + add a conceptual figure

**What's needed:** A short methodological/framework passage — likely a new subsection near the end of the theoretical section (e.g., §2.5 or opening of §3) — that does three things explicitly:
- Names what each thinker contributes on its own: Goffman → the performative/dramaturgical mechanics of self-presentation before an audience; Foucault → the internalization of surveillance and disciplinary power even absent a present observer; Zuboff → the specific character of *data extraction* as a form of behavioral surveillance under surveillance capitalism.
- States what none of the three alone can explain about hybrid/algorithmic work — e.g., Goffman assumes a human, interpretable audience; Foucault's panopticon assumes a fixed, if internalized, disciplinary gaze; Zuboff's account is economic/extractive but under-theorizes the *performative* experience of the surveilled subject. The synthesis is needed because algorithmic monitoring produces an audience that is simultaneously present (data is collected, consequences follow) and absent (non-addressable, non-interpretive, non-reciprocal) — which is precisely the condition the chapter names "hybrid visibility."
